What if the barrier between a curious student and a published discovery was just a piece of software?

Scientific research papers are one of the most valuable sources of knowledge, allowing scientists and researchers to communicate new discoveries that develop technology and deepen our understanding of the world. However, they are often difficult to understand for sixth-form students, such as me, who want to understand a topic more deeply. This therefore makes these papers inaccessible to some students, creating a barrier between students and current researchers. One problem I would like to solve is making scientific research papers more accessible to students through a piece of software that simplifies and organises complex information.

Although I have only recently started learning to code, I can already see how programming could be used to tackle this challenge. A program could allow a user to upload a research paper in PDF format before extracting the text and analysing its contents. It could identify important sections such as the abstract, methodology, results and conclusionThe standard structural sections of a research paper, each carrying a different kind of information., then reorganise the information into a clearer and more structured format. The software could also search for difficult scientific terms within the document and display their definitions alongside the textLike a built-in glossary that appears exactly where you need it, instead of a separate search., reducing the need for students to constantly search for unfamiliar vocabulary themselves.

The program could also identify the key ideas within each section and generate concise summaries that highlight the most important information. Rather than reading an entire paper several times, students could first gain an overview before exploring the original research in greater depth. Other features could also be included such as automatically generating revision notes, producing simple diagrams to explain scientific processes, or allowing users to highlight sections and save personalised notes.

Creating a project like this would require me to learn many areas of programming, including reading and processing documents, organising large amounts of text, designing an intuitive user interface, and creating algorithms capable of recognising patterns within written information. While these are skills I have not yet developed, learning them would not only help me build this project but also strengthen my understanding of software development and problem-solving.

Ultimately, my goal would not be to replace scientific papers but to make them more approachable for students who are eager to learn. By combining programming with my passion for science, I hope that one day I will be able to develop software that encourages more students to engage with real scientific research and discover ideas that extend far beyond the classroom.

Features sketched out in the essay

Input

Upload a research paper as a PDF; the software extracts and reads its text.

Structure

Detects abstract, methodology, results and conclusion, then reorganises them clearly.

Glossary

Flags difficult scientific terms and shows plain-English definitions alongside the text.

Summaries

Generates concise, section-by-section summaries of the key ideas.

Revision

Automatically produces revision notes from the paper's content.

Notes

Lets students highlight sections and save personalised notes and diagrams.

Not to replace the paper — to open the door to it.
